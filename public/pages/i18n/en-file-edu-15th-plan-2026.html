I. Document Overview

The 15th Five-Year Plan for Education Development is a national-level special education plan issued by the State Council, formulated in accordance with the Outline of the 15th Five-Year Plan for National Economic and Social Development and the Outline for Building a Leading Education Nation (2024–2035). It sets out the development direction, strategic tasks, planning layout, and reform measures for China's education sector during the 2026–2030 period.

Core objective: By 2030, substantial progress shall be made in building a leading education nation, and a high-quality education system shall be basically established. This encompasses five dimensions — the Party's overall leadership over education is further strengthened; the effect of fostering virtue through education becomes more pronounced; the public's sense of gain from education is significantly enhanced; education's support for science, technology, and talent becomes more robust; and the international influence, competitiveness, and voice of China's education rise markedly.

📌 Note: This is the publicly released version. The original text is marked "abridged," and some internal implementation details are not disclosed.

II. Overall Framework: the 5+3+6 System

The main body of the plan consists of 5 strategic tasks, 3 planning layouts, and 6 comprehensive reforms, forming a complete closed loop from blueprint to implementation, and from hardware to institutions.

Five Strategic Tasks

🌱 1. Shaping a New Paradigm for Fostering Virtue Through Education

Integrated reform of ideological and political courses; healthy school initiatives (≥2h of daily physical activity, 15-minute breaks); AI education across all educational stages; cultivation of critical thinking and innovative capacity. Articles 1–5.

🔬 2. Strengthening Education's Support for Science, Technology, and Talent

Expansion of "Double First-Class" initiative with +100,000 undergraduate slots; "Double Excellence" application-oriented undergraduate programs; "New Double High" vocational education; national interdisciplinary centers; cultivation of top-tier innovative talent. Articles 6–11.

🏫 3. Comprehensively Improving Public Education Services

Inclusive preschool education; balanced compulsory education (exploring extended duration); diversified high schools (comprehensive / science high schools); equal rights for children of migrant workers; employment support for college graduates. Articles 12–17.

👩‍🏫 4. Cultivating a High-Caliber Teaching Workforce

Strengthening teachers with the ethos of educators; coordinated quality improvement in normal (teacher) education; graduate-level teacher preparation; stronger salary and benefits protections; reducing non-teaching burdens. Articles 18–20.

🌍 5. Expanding High-Level Opening-Up of Education to the World

Improving a full-chain study-abroad service system; strengthening the "Study in China" brand; encouraging renowned foreign universities of science and engineering to operate cooperatively in China; participating in global education governance. Articles 21–23.

Three Planning Layouts

(1) Forward-looking layout for school-age population: Establish a school-age population forecasting and early-warning mechanism; advance the quality improvement and expansion of higher education; increase "Double First-Class" undergraduate admissions by more than 100,000; build new types of research-oriented universities.

(2) Regional education layout: Support the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region, the Yangtze River Delta, and the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area in building world-class regional higher-education hubs; promote innovation-driven development of universities relocated to the Xiong'an New Area; implement the plan to revitalize higher education in central and western regions.

(3) Talent cultivation layout: Establish an inter-ministerial coordination mechanism for aligning talent supply and demand; deploy urgently needed disciplines and programs through extraordinary measures; link employment with enrollment planning (red/yellow-card warning system); foster integrated bachelor's–master's–doctoral cultivation.

Six Comprehensive Reforms

① Establishing a sound philosophy of education (breaking away from over-reliance on test scores, prestigious schools, and academic credentials) → ② Integrated coordination of education, science & technology, and talent → ③ Optimizing education management and operating mechanisms → ④ Deepening education evaluation reform (balanced assignment of seats for high-school entrance; diversified admission for college entrance) → ⑤ Digital and intelligent transformation of education ("AI + Education" initiative) → ⑥ Strategic investment mechanism (fiscal education spending > 4% of GDP) and legal safeguards (codification of an education code).

IV. Five Key Findings

Finding 1

AI and digital-intelligent transformation emerge as the overarching theme throughout the document. The "AI + Education" initiative is listed as an independent reform section, covering AI education across all stages, digital-intelligent empowerment of evaluation, a national AI education application pilot base, digital Chinese-language development, and AI ethics and safety oversight. This marks the most extensive mention of artificial intelligence in any successive five-year education plan, representing a long-term structural boon for students in CS/AI fields.

Finding 2

Universities shift from "discipline-centered" to "serving the national strategy." The plan explicitly requires "guiding universities to shift from emphasizing disciplinary development toward serving the national strategy," encourages extraordinary deployment of urgently needed disciplines and programs, and tilts admission quotas toward units undertaking major science and technology missions. This means nationally strategic priority fields (chips, AI, new energy, biomedicine, quantum, etc.) will receive preferential resource allocation and talent support.

Finding 3

Opening-up of education is upgraded in quality, with two-way smooth flow for study abroad. For the first time in a five-year plan, both "improving the full-chain study-abroad service system" and the "Study in China" brand are advanced in parallel. At the same time, renowned foreign universities of science and engineering are encouraged to operate cooperatively in China, signaling continued expansion of Sino-foreign cooperative education programs. For students planning to study abroad, the policy environment is steadily improving; for those considering domestic paths, cooperative education offers a new "internationalization without leaving the country" option.

Finding 4

Differentiated development and diversified admission, breaking "over-reliance on test scores, prestigious schools, and credentials." The plan defines three university development tracks: ① "Double First-Class" (research-oriented), ② application-oriented "Double Excellence," ③ vocational "New Double High"; while advancing balanced assignment/registration-based admission for high-school entrance, diversified college admission, and vocational-education college entrance exams. Note, however — this domestic logic does not directly apply to studying abroad; overseas institutions still weigh hard metrics such as GPA, standardized tests, and language proficiency.

Finding 5

Dual safeguards of investment and the rule of law yield high implementation certainty. The statutory share of fiscal education spending in GDP is maintained above 4%, while for the first time the codification of an education code and revision of the Teacher Law are launched. This combination of "fiscal rigid constraints + top-level legal design" significantly raises the certainty of the plan's implementation — it is not an ordinary "aspirational document."

V. Implications for Further-Education Planning

Although this plan targets the domestic education system, it offers important reference value for students planning to study in Canada or Australia:

1. The CS/AI direction is confirmed, fully aligned with both national strategy and global trends. The plan elevates all-stage AI education to a national policy, and QS reports also show continued growth in CS-related roles in the AI era. Your CS direction overlaps with these dual trends — consider stacking interdisciplinary tracks (CS + Psychology / CS + Data Analytics) to build composite competitiveness.

2. The study-abroad policy environment is stable; the "full-chain study-abroad service system" is a positive signal. For the first time at the national level, study-abroad services are systematically guaranteed, meaning visa counseling, overseas safety, and credential recognition upon return will become more standardized. Your Canada/Australia application path carries no policy risk.

3. Language hard thresholds cannot be bypassed. The plan emphasizes "national language capacity" and "AI literacy," but the logic of studying abroad differs — an IELTS listening/reading score of 4.5 is currently your biggest weakness and the foundational ability for applications and future academics; it must be tackled first (6.5 is the threshold, with listening/reading each 1.5 points short).

4. Alternative options are now richer."Double First-Class expansion with +100,000 undergraduate slots" + the rise of new research-oriented universities + Sino-foreign cooperative education (encouraging foreign science/engineering universities to enter China) form a diversified further-education portfolio. But your primary path is clear (Canada/Australia), so treat these as Plan B references only.

5. Critical thinking and soft skills are long-term competitive advantages. The plan repeatedly stresses critical thinking, innovative capacity, and interdisciplinary cultivation, corroborating QS reports (Canada's cognitive-skills gap of −9.6). During your studies abroad, consciously cultivating these "AI-irreplaceable" abilities will become a differentiating advantage in the future job market.
